How to read the map

One cell is one IPv4 address. The cells do not run line by line like text: they follow a Z-order (Morton) curve. The first half of a prefix is the left or top half of its map, the first quarter is the top-left quarter, and so on down to single addresses.

  • Every aligned subnet is a rectangle: a /24 is a 16 × 16 square on a /16 map, a /25 is half of it, a /26 a quarter.
  • Neighbouring subnets are neighbouring rectangles, but two cells side by side are not always consecutive addresses.
  • An odd prefix length (/17, /23) gives two squares side by side: the second half of the range is the right square.
  • Grey numbers are the octet that tells the 16 × 16 squares apart, e.g. the third octet of each /24 on a /16 or /20 map. They are drawn on free cells only.
  • Prefixes shorter than /16 get one cell per block (a /24 on a /8 map). Darker means more IP addresses in the block; a checkerboard means smaller prefixes inside it.

Colours

  • Fill: the tenant of the innermost child prefix. White: no child prefix, or one without a tenant. Dotted lines are prefix boundaries.
  • IP addresses: black in use; dark grey DHCP or SLAAC; light grey reserved or deprecated; faint grey inactive; red with a role (anycast, VIP, …).
  • Usage bars: addresses with an IP record out of all addresses, counted through any nested prefixes, containers included. Over 80% is drawn in orange.

Free space

  • An address is free when it has no IP record and no child prefix covers it.
  • The network and broadcast addresses of a subnet are not free. In pools, containers, /31 and /32 they are, as in NetBox.
  • Child containers are transparent: space not assigned inside them counts as free in their parent.
  • Free blocks are aligned: the inspector lists the largest ones, or the first ones of a chosen size by address.

Data

  • Maps are drawn from NetBox and redrawn when NetBox reports a change through its webhook. The dot in the top bar turns amber when the last update failed or the data is older than 24 hours.
  • A container in the Global VRF spans all VRFs, as in NetBox. When one address exists in several VRFs, the record with a role is shown, then an active one, then reserved, then inactive.
  • IPv4 only.

Working with the map

Hover, clickInspect an address; click pins it, the URL then links to it
Double-click, EnterOpen the child prefix map, or the IP address in NetBox
← → ↑ ↓Move the pin; with Shift, by 16 cells
cCopy the pinned address
EscUnpin
/Search an address or prefix in all VRFs
+ − 0Zoom in, out, fit
[ ]Show or hide the prefix tree and the inspector
PrintPrints the map alone, as vector graphics with title and legend; "Download SVG" in the inspector saves the same drawing
